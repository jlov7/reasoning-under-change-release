# Limitations

Retained benchmark tasks and scenarios are authored synthetic material.
The work planner also accepts user-supplied queues; these are outside the
research protocols and do not establish utility or external scheduling validity. The changing-grid
proposer gains no planning quality from additional simulated compute. Its
negative result concerns the scripted workload and heuristics, not reasoning
models. Notice delivery is complete, instantaneous and free; real notification
latency, missed events and observation costs are unmeasured.

The static planning tasks are one small capacity-constrained family. V1's seed
holdout remains historical within that family. V2 reuses every exposed task;
it is not a new holdout. Five timing repetitions do not create five independent
tasks. DP/full-search quality parity and observed host timings must be reported
separately. Different work units and common ceilings are not matched compute.
Timeouts are cooperative checks between bounded operations, not hard deadlines.

Work counterfactuals and capacity reports optimize the supplied additive values
under the main capacity and up to four optional named budgets. Each resource
uses one consistent unit; unlike units are never combined. Frontier varies only
main capacity while named budgets remain fixed, and scenarios preserve named
profiles/limits. Exact resource vectors may retain many states, so maximum-size
requests can exceed the shared cooperative deadline and fail without a result.
Resource-unit/value-basis metadata records assumptions; it
cannot validate estimates or prevent an operator declaring inconsistent units.
History checking establishes an anchored supplied sequence's internal consistency.
The unchanged unsigned plan format binds only predecessor input, not predecessor
plan bytes. Replaced consistent histories and alternative ancestry converging on
the same input remain indistinguishable. Reported completions remain assertions.

The reader checks internal consistency and deterministic outcomes. It cannot
prove host timing authenticity, scientific generalization or usefulness to a
person. Hashes provide byte identity, not authorship or approval.
